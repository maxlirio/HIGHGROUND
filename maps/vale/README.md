# Vale of Harrow — map data

Generated by `tools/map/landform.py` (numpy only: `python3 tools/map/landform.py`, ~5–10 min).
Validate with `python3 tools/map/check_vale.py` (no land pits, buildable towns, shallow fords).
Deterministic (seed 1307). Do not hand-edit the outputs; change the script and regenerate.

## Coordinate system

- 4000 × 4000 m, **1025 × 1025 samples**, sample spacing **3.90625 m** (`size_m / (res-1)`).
  Samples sit on the grid *nodes*: sample (i, j) is at world `x = i*3.90625`, `y = j*3.90625`.
- World origin = **SW corner** of the map. **+x = east, +y = north**, z up, all in metres.
  This matches `js/sim/map.js` (`makeMap` / `loadMap`). (three.js rendering typically uses
  x = east, z = −north; convert at the render layer.)
- Heights are metres above an arbitrary datum (no sea on this map). Range is in `meta.json`
  (`height_min_m` ≈ 43 m at the river exit, `height_max_m` ≈ 194 m on Blackfell).

### Row order — read this before loading anything

| file | row 0 is | index |
|---|---|---|
| `height.f32`, `water.f32` | **SOUTH edge (y = 0)** | `k = j*1025 + i`, row j = world y (south→north), column i = world x (west→east) |
| every `.png` | **NORTH edge (y = 4000)** — north-up images | pixel (col c, row r) ↔ `i = c`, `j = 1024 − r` |

So a PNG pixel row must be flipped (`j = 1024 − r`) to line up with the `.f32` grids.
If you load a PNG as a three.js texture with the default `flipY = true`, v = 0 is the image
bottom = south, which lines up with world y without further flipping.

## Features (`meta.json.features`)

Each entry: `name`, `type`, `xy_m` (world metres, SW origin), `grid_ij` (i = column west→east,
j = row south→north, i.e. the `.f32` index), `z_m`, and a one-line tactical `note`.

Blue side (south/west of the river):
- **Ashby** (blue town, ~(820, 800), ~102 m): low rise with a gentle dome, p95 slope 2.6°; Wychwood Brook ~450 m east.
- **Harrow Ridge**: the dominant ridge, NE–SW from ~(1550, 360) to ~(2620, 1380), crest ~160 m (about 105 m above the bridge).
  Steep NW **forward slope** faces the bridge; long gentle SE **reverse slope** (dead ground to the red bank).
- **Harrow Field**: open cavalry plain, < 3°, between the reverse slope and the lower river.
- **Wychwood Bottom**: deep tributary valley running north to the river (intended woodland).
- **Holloway**: sunken lane, 3.2 m deep, 5 m wide, with hedge-bank lips, curving from the
  farmland toward the bridge site.
- **Ashby farmland**: rolling fields around the town.

The river and its crossings:
- **River Harrow**: enters the W edge at ~(0, 3380), exits the S edge at ~(3780, 0). Meandering,
  ~23 m wide, 1.5–2.5 m deep (unfordable), water surface falls 64 → 45 m.
- **Hob's Ford** (850, 2860) and **Stane Ford** (3050, 1360): ~46 m wide, ~0.4 m deep gravel riffles
  with gentle ramps.
- **Harrow Bridge site** (2100, 2000): narrow (16 m), deep reach with firm raised banks (abutments).
- **Harrow Moss**: floodplain marsh below the bridge. Smaller marshes lie at the NW river entry and on the mere's inflow.

Red side (north/east of the river):
- **Rookham** (red town, ~(3300, 3250), ~120 m): low rise, p95 slope 5.1°, Rook Beck ~250 m to the SE.
- **Tor Knap**: steep isolated motte-like hill at (2650, 2470) with a flat summit for the **ruin**.
- **Gallows Gap**: a narrow defile between **Brant Hill** and **Cold Knowe**, on the route from Stane Ford to Rookham.
- **Harrow Mere**: a lake in a glacial bowl held by a moraine. Its level is its natural spill height.
  It (level 89.5 m) drains south through **Crake Gill**, a deep narrow gully, to the river just upstream of the bridge.
  **Mere Beck** feeds it from the north edge.
- **Blackfell**: rocky upland in the NW with bedded crags and scree.
- **Rook Beck**: a shallow valley past Rookham to the Moss. **Hanger Knoll**: a small wooded knoll.
- **Rookham farmland**: rolling fields.

## Method (summary)

1. Hand-placed macro landforms on a domain-warped fBm base that falls toward the river everywhere.
2. Fluvial erosion: stream-power law, implicit Braun–Willett solver, with multiple-flow-direction
   drainage area, priority-flood routing and hillslope diffusion, run at half resolution.
3. Hydraulic erosion: about 150k vectorised rain droplets with a sediment-capacity model, at full resolution.
4. Thermal erosion: talus relaxation (34° on soil, 40° on rock, 36° on the Knap) forms the scree aprons.
5. Hydrology: the river is carved on its meander line with a monotone water surface, then the fords, bridge,
   becks, mere, marsh (height above drainage) and sunken lane. A final priority-flood leaves every land cell
   draining to a map edge.

## Land use, vegetation & canopy (`tools/map/landuse_vale.py`)

Run `python3 tools/map/landuse_vale.py` (numpy only, ~2 min, seed 1307) **after** `landform.py` and
`settle_vale.py` — it reads `settlements.json` (roads, reserved footprints, hamlets, woodland hints)
when present and falls back to the two towns if not. Rerun it whenever the terrain or settlements change.

| file | format | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `surface.u8` | uint8, 1025², **south-first** (same order as `height.f32`) | surface id = index into `meta.json.surfaceKeys` (keys of `TERRAIN` in `js/sim/terrain-types.js`; only the keys actually used, in TERRAIN order). Loaded by `loadMap` → `map.surfaceAt(x, y)`. |
| `canopy.u8` | uint8, 1025², south-first | top of woody / tall vegetation in **metres above ground** (0 = none): high forest 17–24, alder carr ~12, coppice ~5, orchards ~5, hedgerows ~3.5 (lone oak standards 12–18), reeds ~2.6, bramble/gorse ~2. For LOS. |
| `vegetation.json` | JSON | `{version, coords, assets, counts, instances:[{asset,x,y,rot,scale}]}` world metres, `rot` radians CCW from +x. Assets: `oak_a/b/c`, `oak_stump` (exist in `assets/glb`), `pine`, `birch`, `alder`, `willow`, `dead_tree`, `fruit_tree`, `bush`, `hedge_shrub`, `log` (no model yet). ≈50k instances; no instance on water, roads or reserved footprints. |
| `landuse_overview.png` | RGB, north-up, 2× grid + legend | surfaces in their TERRAIN `color`, hillshade, water, one dot per tree, labels, coverage legend |

`meta.json.landuse` records the coverage percentages, instance count and which settlements/roads were used.

Model (painting order; later wins): commons (pasture, bracken on slopes, gorse, heath on dry knolls, flower
meadow, bramble) → uplands (Blackfell, Harrow Ridge top, Cold Knowe: moorland, heather, bracken, gorse,
limestone sheep-walk turf, peat) → **hedged closes** ringing each township (pasture, hay, oats, coppice on
slopes, orchards/gardens by the houses) → **open fields**: each township has its own survey axis; great fields
(3 for towns/grange, 2 for hamlets) are sectors of furlongs (~170 × 150–290 m blocks) whose selion strips
(12–22 m, reversed-S) run down the fall line; three-course rotation in its late-summer state (winter field =
wheat with reaped stubble strips, spring field = barley / oats & beans, fallow field = fallow with ploughed
strips); grass headlands; ~35 % of furlong edges hedged → **floodplain** meadows (hay doles, water meadow
where HAND < ~1 m) → **hedgerows** (closes, head-dyke, lanes) → **woods** (Wychwood oakwood in Wychwood Bottom
with a storm-throw deadfall, hanging woods on slopes > ~15°, wooded dingles/gills, Hanger Knoll, the
settlements' `woodland_hints`, pine on the Blackfell flanks, coppices, wood-pasture groves, bramble mantles)
→ marsh family from `marsh.png` (marsh, reed beds by open water, peat bog in the deep bog, alder carr
fringes) → rock (`rock.png` → cliff, scree/boulder field from `scree.png`, rock slab > 33°, limestone
pavement on the Blackfell tops) → river banks (shingle point bars on inner bends, mud on outer, gravel at
the fords) → water (river `deep_water`, riffles `shallow_ford`, becks `stream_bed`, mere + reed fringe) →
settlements (reserved polygons painted with their `surface`, roads by type, hedged lanes through farmland).

Tactical reading: Harrow Field is kept unhedged (grange strips + common turf — cavalry ground); the
bridge approaches are open meadow/pasture; Wychwood, the knoll and gill woods and the hedged closes are
the covered approaches; Harrow Moss (reeds/bog/carr) stops horse.

## Settlements, roads & resources (`tools/map/settle_vale.py`)

Generated by `python3 tools/map/settle_vale.py` (numpy only, ~1 min, seed 1349). It reads the
landform outputs above, so **re-run it whenever the terrain is regenerated**. Do not hand-edit.
`rot` everywhere = radians counter-clockwise from +x (east) about +z; a model's long axis is its local +x.
Polygons are `[[x, y], …]` in world metres, counter-clockwise.

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| `settlements.json` | `towns`, `hamlets`, `sites`, `roads`, `bridges`, `fords`, `reserved`, `woodland_hints`, `fairness`, `contract` |
| `objects.json` | `{objects: [...]}`: every placed model and resource node: `{id, kind, asset, x, y, z, rot, scale, category, res?, amount?, team?, note?}` |
| `settle_overview.png` | 2050² north-up check map: hillshade, roads by type, tofts & crofts, enceintes, resources, labels |

**Towns** (`towns[]`, team 0 = Ashby/blue, 1 = Rookham/red; balanced, not mirrored):
- Ashby is a *green village*: a small green (well in the middle) whose corners the roads leave from.
  Rookham is a *street village*: a main street laid along the contour of its south-facing slope,
  with a market widening on the downhill side.
- `hall`: the starting **town_hall** (manor) on the highest buildable ground near the site, inside a
  `curia` (manor court) polygon. `church` is oriented east–west in a `churchyard`. `mill` is on the
  nearest beck. `well` is on the green or market place.
- `plots[42]`: medieval **tofts** (house yard, street frontage 15–24 m) with **crofts** (long garden
  strips) behind, planted along the last ~300 m of every lane. Each has a house footprint (8×5 m, long
  side to the street or gable-on). `start_house: true` marks the 6 starting houses; the rest are free
  house sites (use them as the build grid for new houses). Two street-front tofts hold the smithy and
  the weaver.
- `enceinte`: suggested wall circuit (closed polyline): a convex oval just outside the core tofts,
  pushed out to the brow of the rise so attackers climb to it, with `gates` where roads cross, and
  labour estimates (palisade 0.6 man-days/m, stone 110 worker-days/m per `economy-research.md`).

**Roads** (`roads[]`: `{id, route, type, width, pts, length_m}`): least-cost A* paths on a 10 m
grid (grade² for carts, cross-slope, marsh, wading, a little low-frequency noise so they don't run
ruler-straight), crossing the river only at the fords and the bridge. Types: `highway` (5 m),
`track` (3–3.5 m), `village_street` (5–7 m), `hollow_way` (the Holloway, 5 m), `causeway` (where a
road crosses marsh ≥ 40), `path` (2 m). Stretches shared by two routes are emitted once.
Key facts: Wychwood Bottom is a gorge (~29° sides), so **all Ashby's wheeled traffic east crosses it
at one point on the Bridge Road** (a chokepoint); Stane Street forks at the Holloway's mouth and runs
along the Moss lip to Harrow Grange, Stane Ford and the Gallows Gap.

**Crossings**: `bridges[0]` = Harrow Bridge (timber, `bridge_wood`, upgradable to `bridge_stone`)
with abutment points and deck heights; `fords[]` = Hob's and Stane Fords (banks, depth, river axis)
plus the small beck splashes where roads wade streams.

**Hamlets & sites**: Wychcote (blue, safe), Harrow Grange (blue, rich but open), Beckfoot (red, safe),
Hobsgarth (red bank, one ford from Ashby); Crake Mill (contested watermill), Tor Knap Keep (ruin),
Gallows at the Gap, St Hob's Chapel. Each hamlet has a `yard` (reserved) and an `infield_hint` ring.

**Resources** (`category: "resource"` in `objects.json`; `res` + `amount` in the sim's units:
kg, silver/gold in d, mana units). Each carries a `note` with its dilemma and `access_cost_m`
(travel effort from each town: route metres weighted by grade, marsh and wading). `fairness` in
`settlements.json` sums amount shares by resource. Gold (Blackfell, above Hob's Ford) is even;
red leads on silver (both veins are north of the river, Cold Knowe's faces Stane Ford) and fish;
blue leads on iron (ridge ironstone + Moss bog-ore) and holds the richest arable (Harrow Grange);
stone, timber and mana are within ±5 %.

**Contract with land use**: leave every `reserved` polygon clear of trees/crops/hedges (paint its
`surface`); paint roads at their width; forest the `woodland_hints` (lumber_site markers sit on their
town-facing edges); arable on hamlet `infield_hint` rings; crofts are kitchen gardens.

`js/sim/resources.js` loads `objects.json`; entries with `category: "structure"` are skipped (so
standing stones, fences and bridges are not mistaken for ley/wood/stone nodes). `gold`, `deer_herd`
and `forage` (berries) have no sim node kind yet and are ignored until the economy adds them.
